A. Experienced solution/distributed-systems architect: recent responsibility for production consistency, integration, incident recovery and architecture decisions. Ask for relevant experience and competing commercial interests; a job title alone is insufficient.
B. Experienced security/enterprise architect: responsibility for identity and data-access design, migration controls, enterprise governance and organisational change. If no person covers both areas, split the assignment rather than implying expertise.
C. Academic: researcher or university teacher in software architecture, distributed systems or information systems/enterprise architecture, able to examine primary literature and assess teaching quality. Declare areas outside their expertise and route those findings to the appropriate specialist.

Each reviewer should disclose prior involvement in writing these books, relevant vendor relationships, limitations of expertise and exact review scope. Reviewers work independently before comparing conclusions. A disagreement is evidence to resolve, not a vote to average.

FINDING FORMAT AND SEVERITY
Use External_Findings.csv, one finding per row. Identify book, chapter/section, PDF page, paragraph or figure, claim ID if applicable, issue, source/evidence, consequence, proposed correction and a way to verify the correction.
Critical: could teach an unsafe design or invalidate a central conclusion.
Major: materially misteaches a concept, unsupported generalisation, incorrect worked solution or a missing assumption changing the decision.
Minor: local precision, citation or presentation defect.
Suggestion: optional enrichment with no demonstrated defect.
Positive observation: a specific checked property, with evidence and scope.
Do not inflate the result with a numerical quality score or an unexplained 'approved'.

REVISION AND ACCEPTANCE
1. Preserve the reviewed PDF/DOCX hashes and register the reviewer and scope.
2. Triage each finding; reproduce it or inspect the cited passage. Record accept/reject/defer with rationale. Never delete disagreement silently.
3. Apply accepted changes to the source; update associated examples, figures, answers, references and claim records together.
4. Ask the relevant reviewer to check critical and major corrections. Record evidence of closure, not just author acknowledgement.
5. Incorporate learner-pilot results and re-run affected content checks.
6. Rebuild DOCX/PDF, update contents and claim-page mappings, and inspect every changed page plus affected neighbours. If pagination shifts widely, inspect the full render.
7. Publish a final edition only with a truthful scope statement. Unresolved critical findings block release. Unresolved major findings require correction or an explicit limitation that the owner accepts; a source-access limitation cannot be described as completed verification.
