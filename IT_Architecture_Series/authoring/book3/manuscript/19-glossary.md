# Glossary

This glossary collects the technical terms used in the book. Each entry gives the meaning used here; the chapters show the terms at work in the Helios case.

**ADKAR.** Hiatt's model of individual change: awareness, desire, knowledge, ability and reinforcement, achieved in order. The first weak element is where to act.

**Alignment perspective.** In the strategic alignment model, a path that starts from one strategy domain, works through a second domain and changes a third, such as strategy execution or competitive potential.

**Application portfolio.** The set of applications an organisation runs, managed as a group of investments with recorded purpose, owner, cost, dependencies and lifecycle status.

**ArchiMate.** The Open Group's modelling language for enterprise architecture. Version 4 organises elements into domains and aspects and uses one set of behaviour elements across the business, application and technology domains.

**Architecture.** The fundamental concepts or properties of a system or enterprise in its environment, together with the principles that govern its realisation and evolution.

**Architecture board.** A cross-unit body that reviews significant designs and approves standards and exceptions within delegated authority, sending investment and risk decisions to their accountable owners.

**Architecture description.** A work product, such as a set of views and models, that expresses an architecture for its stakeholders.

**Architecture Development Method (ADM).** TOGAF's cycle of phases, from the preliminary phase and architecture vision to architecture change management, around requirements management.

**Architecture principle.** A rule that guides architecture decisions, written with a name, statement, rationale and implications. A useful principle is one that a real proposal could break.

**Aspect.** In ArchiMate, the classification of an element as active structure (performs behaviour), behaviour (the work done) or passive structure (acted upon).

**Assurance.** Justified confidence that controls work as intended, based on current evidence with a stated scope and date.

**Authoritative source.** The system whose value wins when copies of a specific business fact disagree.

**Benefit chain.** A sequence of observable steps from an intervention to a benefit, each with an owner and a measure.

**Benefits register.** A record of each expected benefit with its baseline, mechanism, measure, owner, timing, type and evidence of realisation.

**Bimodal IT.** An organisation of IT into a stability-oriented mode and a speed-oriented mode, each with its own practices.

**Business capability.** Something an organisation is able to do to achieve a purpose, described independently of process, application and organisational unit.

**Capability heat map.** A capability map with separate ratings for importance, effectiveness and confidence, each with a recorded basis.

**Capability map.** A hierarchy of business capabilities, usually two levels deep, used to compare investments across reorganisations and system changes.

**Capability-based planning.** Planning and funding change by capability increments, with projects as the means of delivering them.

**COBIT 2019.** ISACA's framework for the governance and management of enterprise information and technology, with five governance objectives, 35 management objectives and design factors for tailoring.

**Common-mode failure.** A single failure, such as a shared identity configuration or control plane, that affects many components at once.

**Conway's law.** The observation that organisations design systems whose structure copies their communication structure.

**Critical path.** The longest chain of dependent activities in a schedule, which sets the earliest completion date.

**Customer identity resolution.** Deciding which records in different systems describe the same customer organisation, site or contact, and recording the links with their match status.

**Data contract.** A published agreement about a dataset's meaning, grain, identifiers, freshness, quality, access, change policy and support.

**Data owner.** The person accountable for the meaning, approved uses and quality decisions of a business fact.

**Data steward.** The person who maintains definitions, resolves day-to-day data issues such as proposed matches, and coordinates corrections.

**Decision rights.** The right to make a decision, as distinct from input rights, the right to be consulted.

**Design factor.** In COBIT 2019, a characteristic of the enterprise, such as its risk profile or sourcing model, that changes the priority of governance and management objectives.

**Difference-in-differences.** An estimate of an intervention's effect: the change in the treated group minus the change in a comparison group over the same period.

**Digital platform.** A set of reusable business, data and technology components from which teams can quickly assemble and change digital offerings.

**Domain.** In ArchiMate 4, a grouping of elements: common, business, application, technology, strategy, motivation, and implementation and migration.

**DORA.** Either the DevOps Research and Assessment programme and its software delivery measures, or the EU's Digital Operational Resilience Act for financial entities. The context decides which.

**Enterprise architecture.** The description of how capabilities, processes, information, applications, technology and decision rights fit together, used to choose and sequence change.

**Enterprise architecture function.** The roles, bodies and processes through which an organisation creates, approves, enforces and observes architecture decisions.

**Exception record.** A governed, time-limited approval to depart from a standard, with reason, scope, owner, risk, operating controls, expiry and path to compliance.

**Expected value of information.** The increase in expected value from making a decision after, rather than before, obtaining information such as a pilot result.

**Exploration and exploitation.** March's distinction between searching for new possibilities and refining what an organisation already knows.

**Federated governance.** An arrangement in which a central team sets shared identifiers, rules and minimum controls while domain teams own the meaning and quality of their data.

**Flow efficiency.** Active time divided by elapsed time for a request moving through a value stream.

**Functional unit.** In the Software Carbon Intensity specification, the unit of work that emissions are expressed per, such as one completed service request.

**Grain.** What a single row of a dataset represents, such as one customer organisation or one pair of linked records.

**Group customer identifier.** Helios's governed identifier for a customer organisation, linking the identifiers used in each source system.

**Internal rate of return (IRR).** The discount rate at which an investment's net present value is zero.

**ISO/IEC 38500.** The international standard for governance of IT. The governing body evaluates, directs and monitors, and the 2024 edition adds engaging stakeholders and adopts the eleven principles of ISO 37000.

**IT governance.** The specification of decision rights and accountability that encourages desirable behaviour in the use of IT.

**Master data.** Core data about the main business entities, such as customers, sites and products, shared by many processes and systems.

**Master data management style.** Where master values live and are changed: registry, consolidation, coexistence or centralised.

**Match status.** The state of a proposed link between records: proposed, verified or rejected.

**Measurement contract.** A written definition of a measure: purpose, owner, population, numerator, denominator, exclusions, timing and known limits.

**Mechanism.** An explanation of how one change could lead to another, through observable intermediate steps.

**Mirroring hypothesis.** The proposition that a product's architecture tends to mirror the structure of the organisation that develops it.

**Multiplicity.** In ArchiMate 4, a constraint on how many element instances may sit at each end of a relationship.

**Net present value (NPV).** The sum of an investment's future cash flows discounted to a single date, minus the investment.

**Nominal and real values.** Nominal amounts are in the money of the year they occur; real amounts are in constant prices. Each must be discounted at the matching rate.

**Operating model.** The level of business process integration and standardisation an organisation needs to deliver its goods and services.

**Operational backbone.** The integrated core systems, data and processes that run the business reliably and efficiently.

**Organisational anchoring.** The extent to which enterprise architecture is embedded in an organisation's decisions and processes.

**Parallel trends.** The assumption, needed for a causal reading of difference-in-differences, that the groups would have moved in parallel without the intervention.

**Payback.** The time until cumulative benefits, discounted or not, repay an investment. The result depends on the timing convention.

**Plateau.** In ArchiMate, a relatively stable state of the architecture, such as a baseline, transition state or target.

**Priority candidate.** In this book's heat-map rule, a capability with high importance, effectiveness of 2 or below and at least medium confidence.

**Real option.** The right, without an obligation, to take a later action on a real investment, such as expanding, delaying or abandoning it.

**Regression to the mean.** The tendency of a group selected for an extreme measurement to move towards its average next time, even without any change.

**Review trigger.** A condition, such as cross-unit impact or a new use of sensitive data, that sends a change for wider review.

**Risk.** In ISO 31000, the effect of uncertainty on objectives. A risk statement names a condition, an event and a consequence.

**Roadmap.** A sequence of operable intermediate states, with the work, dependencies and conditions needed to move between them.

**Seven Rs.** AWS's vocabulary of cloud migration strategies: retire, retain, rehost, relocate, repurchase, replatform and refactor.

**Simpson's paradox.** A reversal in which a pattern within every group disappears or reverses when the groups are combined.

**Slack.** The time an activity can be delayed without delaying the whole schedule.

**Software Carbon Intensity (SCI).** The Green Software Foundation's measure of emissions per functional unit: energy times grid intensity, plus embodied emissions, per unit.

**Solution architect.** An architect who designs a specific change within enterprise principles and domain standards. Enterprise and domain architects work across the organisation and within one area respectively.

**Standardised rate.** A rate recalculated with a fixed reference mix of groups, to separate changes in group performance from changes in mix.

**Strategic alignment model.** Henderson and Venkatraman's model linking business strategy, IT strategy, organisational infrastructure and IT infrastructure through strategic fit and functional integration.

**Technical custodian.** The person or team that operates the storage, access mechanisms and backups for data.

**Technical debt.** The future cost of change created by past design shortcuts.

**TIME model.** A practitioner heuristic, generally attributed to Gartner, that classifies applications by business value and technical fitness as tolerate, invest, migrate or eliminate.

**TOGAF Standard.** The Open Group's framework for developing and governing enterprise architecture, built around the Architecture Development Method.

**Total cost of ownership (TCO).** All the costs of an option over the same period: implementation, migration, operation, support, training, upgrades and exit, including the old system's cost during transition.

**Transaction cost economics.** Williamson's theory of which transactions to govern through markets and which internally, depending on factors such as asset specificity.

**Transition architecture.** In TOGAF, an intermediate state of the architecture between baseline and target.

**Value stream.** The sequence of stages through which value reaches a stakeholder, from trigger to result.

**Vendor lock-in.** Being unable to move away from a supplier without substantial cost.

**View and viewpoint.** A view addresses particular stakeholder concerns; the viewpoint sets the conventions for constructing and reading it.

**Workforce identity and access.** The management of how staff sign in to applications and lose access when they change role or leave.

**Zachman Framework.** Zachman's ontology of enterprise descriptions: six interrogatives as columns and six audience perspectives as rows, 36 cells in version 3.0.

**Zero trust.** A security approach that grants no implicit trust based on network location or ownership, and evaluates each access request against current identity, device state and policy.
