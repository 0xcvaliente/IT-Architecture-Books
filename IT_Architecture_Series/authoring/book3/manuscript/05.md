# 5 Application portfolios and rationalisation

## Learning objectives

After this chapter you should be able to:

- Design an application inventory record that supports investment and retirement decisions.
- Classify applications with the TIME model and explain what the classification leaves out.
- Compare cloud migration strategies for a given application and deadline.
- Evaluate a retirement plan for completeness.

## Inventory for a decision

An **application portfolio** is the set of applications an organisation runs, managed as a group of investments rather than as separate systems. Managing it needs an inventory, and the inventory should hold the facts that decisions depend on. For each application, record:

- business purpose and the capabilities it supports;
- accountable owner, users and non-interactive consumers such as batch jobs;
- the facts it is authoritative for, its interfaces and its dependencies;
- criticality and recovery needs;
- annual operating cost, split into licences, infrastructure, internal support and allocated shared services;
- lifecycle status, contract terms and planned changes;
- the date of each fact and how confident the team is in it.

Old inventories mislead. After acquisitions, supplier changes and departmental development, an inventory that is a year old may miss applications and list others that no longer run.

Helios's forty-three applications include six that represent the main issues. Their annual operating costs are already part of the €12 million group IT baseline; they are not additional spending.

| Application | Main responsibility | Annual operating cost (EUR) | Initial issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| CRM North | Industrial service cases | 420,000 | Weak handoff reconciliation |
| CRM Central | Distribution service cases | 360,000 | Similar customer data, different identifiers |
| ERP Core | Financial and asset records | 900,000 | Shared critical dependency |
| Legacy Orders | Order intake and state | 600,000 | Hosting contract ends in 14 months |
| Analytics Warehouse | Group reporting | 300,000 | Inconsistent metric definitions |

The sample totals €2.82 million. It is a sample, so the average cost per application cannot be extrapolated to the other thirty-seven without data about them. Splitting cost by type matters for savings: retiring an application removes its licence, but shared infrastructure costs may remain unless the platform is resized.

## The TIME model

The **TIME model** is a widely used practitioner heuristic for application portfolios, generally attributed to the analyst firm Gartner. It scores each application on two dimensions:

- **Business value:** how much the application contributes, for example the importance of the capabilities it supports, its usage and whether its functions are unique.
- **Technical fitness:** how sound the application is, for example its supportability, reliability, integration quality and cost to run.

Splitting each dimension at the midpoint of a 1-to-5 scale gives four suggested dispositions, from which the model takes its name. This book counts a score of 3.0 or more as high and below 3.0 as low:

- **Tolerate:** sound but low value. Keep running at minimum cost and avoid new investment.
- **Invest:** valuable and sound. Extend and improve.
- **Migrate:** valuable but technically weak. Move its functions to a better platform or product.
- **Eliminate:** low value and technically weak. Retire when it is safe to do so.

### Worked example: scoring Helios's sample

The architecture team and each application owner score four criteria per dimension from 1 to 5 and take the mean. For CRM North, the business-value criteria are capability importance 4, number of users 3, uniqueness of functions 4 and contribution to revenue 3, giving (4 + 3 + 4 + 3) / 4 = 3.5. The technical-fitness criteria are supportability 2, reliability 2, integration quality 3 and running-cost efficiency 1, giving (2 + 2 + 3 + 1) / 4 = 2.0. CRM North therefore falls in the Migrate quadrant. {FIG:time_quadrant} plots all six applications.

TIME is useful because it is quick, visual and easy to explain to a board. Its limits are just as important. The scores are judgements, and an application near a boundary, such as CRM Central, can change quadrant with one criterion. The model also ignores dependencies, contracts, migration cost and timing. A quadrant suggests a disposition; the business case, the dependency analysis and the exit terms decide it.

## Dispositions and migration strategies

Portfolio decisions use a wider vocabulary than TIME's four words. An application can be retained, improved, consolidated with another, replaced, moved to new hosting or retired. When the decision involves moving to the cloud, AWS describes seven common migration strategies, often called the **seven Rs** {REF:sevenrs}:

- **Retire:** switch the application off.
- **Retain:** keep it where it is for now and revisit later.
- **Rehost:** move it to the cloud without changing it ("lift and shift").
- **Relocate:** move it to the cloud at the virtualisation level, without changing the application or its operating system.
- **Repurchase:** replace it with a different product, often software as a service.
- **Replatform:** move it with a few targeted changes, such as using a managed database.
- **Refactor:** redesign it to use cloud-native features.

### Worked example: Legacy Orders

Legacy Orders falls in Migrate, and its hosting contract ends in 14 months. The team estimates four options.

| Strategy | One-off cost and duration | Margin before the deadline | Main consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rehost | €0.3m over 4 months | 10 months | Keeps its technical weaknesses |
| Replatform | €0.6m over 8 months | 6 months | Managed database cuts support effort |
| Repurchase | €0.9m over 12 months | 2 months | New subscription cost; process change for staff |
| Refactor | €1.8m over 18 months | −4 months | Misses the deadline without a hosting extension |

Refactoring would produce the best long-term design, but it needs four months more than the deadline allows, so it is only feasible with a hosting extension. Repurchase leaves only two months of margin for a project that also changes staff processes, which is little room for the usual slippage. Replatform meets the deadline with six months to spare and reduces running effort; rehost is cheaper but leaves the technical problems in place. A defensible recommendation is to replatform now and to reconsider repurchase when the subscription market and Helios's order processes are better understood. The €0.6 million comes from the legacy modernisation work package, so it must be shown there, not as new money.

## Application counts are poor success measures

Reducing forty-three applications to thirty may still increase cost if the remaining products are more expensive and integration work grows. Conversely, a new shared service can increase the count while removing duplicated logic and risk. Measure the outcome the rationalisation is meant to create: lower run cost, fewer reconciliation hours, fewer failed handoffs or reduced exposure to unsupported software.

## Worked comparison: two CRMs

CRM North and CRM Central both support service cases, but their fulfilment processes differ. There are three main options:

1. Keep both CRMs, with common customer identifiers and reliable integration between them.
2. Consolidate onto one product while preserving the process variations that are genuinely needed.
3. Replace both as part of a broader process redesign.

The first option delivers shared-data benefits sooner but keeps two systems to run. The second may reduce platform cost but needs migration, a good process fit and a tested way of separating each division's access. The third gives the most design freedom with the largest scope and adoption risk. Licence price alone cannot rank them.

Before selecting, test representative workflows and exports. Include attachments, case history, identifiers and audit records. A demonstration of a new case form shows only that the form works. It does not show that the product can carry over the existing business history or support the exception process.

## Retirement is a project

A system marked "retired" in the inventory while it still runs, and is still billed, has delivered no saving. A retirement plan needs:

- migrated users and replacement functionality, confirmed by the business owner;
- a decision on records: migrate, archive or keep read-only access, under the retention policy;
- removal of integrations and scheduled jobs, including monthly and annual ones;
- revoked access and decommissioned infrastructure;
- cancelled or reduced contracts, with the notice period in the timeline;
- a watch period for missed dependencies, with a plan for responding if one appears.

Archives need care. A spreadsheet export of current rows may lose relationships, attachments and the meaning of historical codes. Check that the archive answers the questions people will actually ask of it.

## Prioritising the first actions

For Helios, the Legacy Orders deadline is a fixed time constraint, the gaps in the workforce identity lifecycle are a risk across every application, and inconsistent reporting makes it hard to measure the programme at all. These may justify early work even though CRM consolidation promises larger savings later. Sequence investments by dependency and risk as well as by projected benefit.

Record each major disposition in a portfolio decision record: the reason, options considered, supporting data, transition cost, owner, timing and the condition that would trigger a review. This links the enterprise model to funded action.

## Key points

- An application inventory is useful when it records the facts that decisions depend on, with dates and confidence.
- The TIME model classifies applications by business value and technical fitness. It suggests a disposition; cost, dependencies and timing decide it.
- The seven Rs give a vocabulary for cloud migration choices. Compare them against the real deadline, cost and long-term consequence.
- Application counts and licence prices are inputs. Measure the outcomes that rationalisation is meant to improve.
- Retirement delivers savings only when users, data, integrations, access and contracts have all been dealt with.
