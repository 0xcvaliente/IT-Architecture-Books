# 6 Enterprise information and data governance

## Learning objectives

After this chapter you should be able to:

- Assign authority for specific business facts rather than for whole entities.
- Distinguish the roles of data owner, data steward and technical custodian.
- Design a data contract and a correction workflow for shared data.
- Compare central and federated data governance for a given decision.
- Compare the four master data management styles and place a design among them.

## One entity, several authorities

"Customer" can mean a legal organisation, a service site, a billing party, a contact person or a commercial relationship. Different systems can legitimately be authoritative for different facts about the same customer. An **authoritative source** is the system whose value wins when copies disagree, and a **data owner** is the person accountable for that fact's meaning and quality.

Helios's first fact-authority table looks like this:

| Fact | Authoritative source | Owner | Who may change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal organisation and registration number | Party register | Group data owner | Party stewards |
| Billing account and invoices | ERP Core | Finance director | Finance operations |
| Staff credentials and access lifecycle | Identity Platform | Security officer | Identity service only |
| Service-case progress | The CRM handling the case | Division service manager | Case handlers in that division |
| Link between records in different systems | Customer link hub | Group data owner | Party stewards |

The table settles arguments before they start. When CRM North and ERP Core disagree about a customer's legal name, the party register wins and CRM North's copy is corrected. When they disagree about an invoice, ERP Core wins.

An enterprise data model should make these distinctions explicit. It does not need one enormous customer object stored everywhere. Define the shared identifiers and meanings needed for coordination, and leave local models alone where their meanings genuinely differ. {FIG:authority} shows the pattern.

## Governed identifiers and match status

Helios creates a **group customer identifier** for each customer organisation. It carries the identifiers used in each source system, the organisation's legal relationships, where each link came from and a **match status**:

- **Proposed:** an automated rule suggests two records describe the same organisation.
- **Verified:** a steward has confirmed the link using an agreed check.
- **Rejected:** a steward has confirmed that the records describe different organisations.

For example, CRM North holds "Nordvik Pumps AS" (record N-1042) and CRM Central holds "Nordvik Pumps" (record C-0077). The matching rule scores the pair at 0.86 on name and address similarity, so the link is proposed. A steward checks the company registration number in both records, finds that they agree and marks the link verified. A second pair, "Hallberg Engineering Ltd" and "Hallberg Engineering Services Ltd", scores 0.91 but has two different registration numbers. The steward rejects the link, because these are two legal entities that happen to share a name.

The example shows why a high score is not the same as a verified fact. A merge can expose one customer's information to another, so the resolution process needs both data-quality checks and access-policy controls. An ambiguous match stays proposed until the agreed process resolves it.

## Master data management styles

**Master data** is the core data about the business's main entities, such as customers, sites, products and suppliers, that many processes and systems share. DAMA's body of knowledge for data management treats reference and master data as one of its knowledge areas {REF:dmbok}. Practitioners commonly distinguish four implementation styles for master data, a vocabulary usually attributed to the analyst firm Gartner. The styles differ in where the authoritative values live and where changes are made:

| Style | Where the master values live | Where changes are made | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registry | In the source systems; the hub holds identifiers, links and match status | In the source systems | Linking records across systems with little disruption |
| Consolidation | Copied into a hub that builds a single best record | In the source systems; the hub is read-only | Reporting and analysis |
| Coexistence | In the hub and the source systems, kept in step | In either, synchronised both ways | A gradual move towards shared master data |
| Centralised | In the hub, which is the system of record | Only in the hub; sources read from it | Strict, uniform control of core data |

Helios's customer link hub is a **registry-style** hub. Each CRM stays authoritative for its own records, and the hub holds the group customer identifier, the source identifiers and the match status of each link. The party register adds a centralised element for one narrow set of facts, the legal organisation and its registration number. The registry style is the least disruptive and fits the Coordination operating model of Chapter 4, but it cannot by itself fix a wrong address in a CRM; that still needs a correction in the source. Moving towards coexistence or a centralised hub becomes worth considering only if the divisions later agree common meanings for more customer facts.

## Owners, stewards and custodians

Three roles are easy to blur:

- The **data owner** is accountable for the meaning of the data, its approved uses and the decisions that affect its quality.
- The **data steward** maintains definitions, resolves day-to-day issues such as proposed matches, and coordinates corrections.
- The **technical custodian** operates the storage, access mechanisms and backups.

In a small organisation one person may hold several roles, but the responsibilities remain distinct. A database administrator can restore a table; only the owner can decide whether the restored values are correct.

## Data contracts

A dataset offered to other teams needs a **data contract**: a published agreement about what the data means and what consumers can rely on. Calling a dataset a "data product" does not by itself supply these properties. One element deserves a definition first. The **grain** of a dataset states what a single row represents, for example one customer organisation, one pair of linked records or one service request per day. Two datasets with different grains cannot be joined or compared safely until one is converted to the other's grain. Helios's contract for verified customer links states:

| Element | Verified customer links |
|---|---|
| Meaning | Two source records describe the same legal organisation |
| Grain | One row per pair of linked source records |
| Identifiers | Group customer identifier plus source system and source record identifier |
| Freshness | Available within 15 minutes of verification |
| Quality | Fewer than 5 false links per 1,000 in the monthly audit sample |
| Access | Service agents within their permitted scope; analysts in aggregate |
| Change policy | Additions with 30 days' notice; changes of meaning need data council approval |
| Support | Party stewardship team, business hours, named escalation |

The contract also says what the data cannot be used to establish. A proposed match with a score of 0.86 may be useful to an analyst estimating the number of shared customers, but it is unsuitable for granting a service agent access to another division's cases. Reuse depends on fitness for the consumer's purpose as well as on technical availability.

## Data management as a discipline

The work in this chapter belongs to a wider discipline. The DAMA Data Management Body of Knowledge (DAMA-DMBOK) describes eleven knowledge areas, with **data governance** at the centre guiding the other ten {REF:dmbok}:

- data architecture, and data modelling and design;
- data storage and operations, and data security;
- data integration and interoperability;
- document and content management;
- reference and master data;
- data warehousing and business intelligence;
- metadata management and data quality.

An enterprise architect seldom runs these functions, but needs to know which of them a decision touches. Helios's fact-authority table draws on governance, master data and metadata. The correction workflow later in this chapter draws on data quality, integration and security. Naming the knowledge areas helps the architect find the right specialists and avoid designing a governance process that the data team already runs.

## Central and federated governance

A central team can define shared identifiers, interoperability rules and minimum controls. Domain teams can remain responsible for the meaning and quality of their own data. This **federated** arrangement combines common coordination with local expertise, but it needs a way to resolve disagreements between domains and a way to fund shared work.

Too much centralisation makes the central team a bottleneck for every local schema change. Too little lets incompatible definitions and duplicated sensitive data spread. Choose decision boundaries by who bears the consequence. For example, adding an optional field to a local maintenance form is a domain decision; changing the definition of group revenue requires broader governance.

A data catalogue connects these decisions to actual systems and owners. Its value depends on being current and used. A catalogue entry labelled "trusted" without a contract or an enforcement route tells a consumer very little.

## Correction and lifecycle

Suppose a steward wrongly merges two customers, and the merged identifier has already reached CRM North, CRM Central and the analytics warehouse. A shared identifier that cannot be corrected safely spreads errors faster than separate systems ever did. A correction workflow needs these steps:

1. Pause automated propagation of the affected identifier.
2. Identify every downstream copy and report that used the merged record, using lineage records.
3. Decide the authoritative correction: a split into two identifiers, with the correct source records attached to each.
4. Publish the correction as an event that consumers process, preserving the history of the mistaken link.
5. Reconcile both CRMs and rebuild or annotate affected reports.
6. Review whether the merge disclosed information to people who should not have seen it, and involve the privacy officer if it did.
7. Record the cause and close the case only when every consumer confirms completion.

Lifecycle rules apply across the same copies. The EU General Data Protection Regulation requires organisations to process personal data for specified purposes, to limit it to what is necessary and to build data protection into the design of processing {REF:gdpr}. The exact obligations depend on the processing and on each organisation's role, so Helios's retention, access and transfer policies must come from its privacy and legal specialists. The architecture's job is to make those policies enforceable: a deletion or restriction must reach operational systems, analytical stores, exports and backups, and a restore from backup must not silently undo it.

## Worked first-year data programme

Begin with the small set of customer-organisation facts needed for service coordination and group reporting. Assign authority, define matching and correction workflows, and pilot with two divisions. Measure:

- the number and age of proposed matches awaiting a decision;
- false merges found in audit samples;
- the time taken to complete a correction;
- whether consumers find the links useful for their purpose.

Do not ingest every available personal field simply because the platform can store it. Publish one governed service metric with a declared grain and correction policy before attempting a group-wide dashboard. The enterprise contribution is to settle who owns each definition, who may use it, which divisions must adopt it and how disagreements are resolved.

## Key points

- Assign authority to specific facts rather than whole entities. Different systems can be authoritative for different facts about the same customer.
- Governed identifiers carry a match status. Proposed links are useful for some purposes and dangerous for others, such as granting access. Helios's link hub is a registry-style master data hub.
- Owners decide meaning and use, stewards maintain and correct, custodians operate.
- Data contracts state meaning, grain, freshness, quality, access and change rules, so that consumers know what they can rely on.
- Correction and lifecycle processes must reach every copy, including reports and backups.
