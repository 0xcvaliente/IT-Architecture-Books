# 17 Capstone transformation programme and board defence

## Learning objectives

After this chapter you should be able to:

- Design a three-year transformation programme that integrates enterprise and solution reasoning.
- Construct a dossier in which every board-level commitment can be traced to supporting analysis.
- Revise the programme coherently after a funding cut and an acquisition.
- Defend a conditional recommendation to a board and to a technical panel.

## The assignment

Act as Helios's enterprise architect. Recommend a three-year transformation programme within the €9 million envelope, and explain its relationship to the separate €12 million annual operating baseline. The programme must improve selected capabilities, preserve valid local differences and keep the business running through every transition.

The task is to make a defensible set of commitments rather than to choose one product for every capability. For each commitment, say what will change and why, who owns the consequence, what it costs, what must happen first and how success or failure will be recognised. Where a business promise depends on a specific integration, data or recovery mechanism, show that mechanism in enough technical detail to be checked.

## Required dossier

Keep the board brief short, and put supporting analysis in appendices that can be traced from it. Page counts are guides; a clear model can replace several pages of prose.

| Deliverable | Required content |
|---|---|
| Board decision brief | Two pages: problem, recommendation, commitment, cost, major risks and decisions requested |
| Enterprise baseline | Capability map and heat map, value stream, six representative applications, dependencies, known gaps |
| Operating model and principles | Operating-model choice by capability, architecture principles, tailored method |
| Target and transitions | Data authority, shared and local responsibilities, platform policy, at least two operable intermediate states |
| Governance and assurance | Decision rights, exception process, risk ownership, assurance plan |
| Investment and roadmap | Cash-flow model, benefits register, dependencies, annual constraints, retirement conditions |
| Adoption and sourcing | Changed work, service ownership, supplier eligibility, tested exit scenario |
| Measurement and research | Outcome measures, baseline, comparison and its limits, critical synthesis of sources |
| Technical appendix | One detailed integration or migration design with invariants, failure behaviour and operational evidence |

The six representative applications are a sample of forty-three and leave most of the portfolio unexamined. State what further inventory work is needed before any group-wide retirement commitment. Do not invent interfaces to fill a diagram; label hypotheses and assign discovery work.

## Review gates

The programme passes three gates:

1. **Gate 1, discovery and pilot funding.** Is the problem defined and the scope clear enough to fund discovery and the pilot?
2. **Gate 2, transition approval.** Do the pilot results support the chosen transition?
3. **Gate 3, rollout.** Do service ownership, recovery, data correction and adoption results support wider rollout?

At each gate, name what is approved and what remains conditional. A gate is useful only if it can change the decision, so define in advance the results that would stop, narrow or delay the work. For example, Gate 2 might require identity-related misrouting in the pilot divisions to fall by a stated amount against a comparison division. Keep the cost of delay and the cost of continuing the baseline in view, so that caution is weighed fairly.

## Change card A: funding reduction

After your first complete dossier, reduce the envelope by 20% to €7.2 million. Keep a dated copy of the first version. Produce a revised allocation that sums correctly and update the deferred outcomes, dependencies, benefits, annual cash needs and risks.

Chapter 11's deferral scenario is one possible answer. Challenge its assumption that the deferred legacy systems can keep running safely, and propose another scope choice if that assumption fails. Chapter 10 shows how a cut in the wrong place can destroy most of an investment's value while saving little; check your revised plan for the same effect. Do not silently remove contingency, assurance or support while claiming that the earlier promises still hold.

## Change card B: acquisition

Helios acquires a business that records customer relationships by service site and contract, whereas the pilot links records mainly by legal party. Some sites share a parent company but have different permissions, entitlements and billing arrangements; for example, two depots owned by the same logistics group may hold separate maintenance contracts. The quality of the acquired data has not been measured.

Revise the data model and the integration scope. A match on the parent company must not automatically grant access to every site. Introduce explicit relationships, named authorities and review of uncertain links, then reassess the benefits and the migration schedule. The acquisition may strengthen the case for shared identity meanings while weakening the case for early process consolidation.

Neither the initial €9 million nor the revised €7.2 million automatically includes acquisition integration. State whether you absorb a defined scope by deferring other work, or request a separate investment decision. Either can be defensible if the costs, dependencies and consequences are explicit.

## Assessment guide

The weights below are a course rubric for reviewing your submission. A different design can earn full credit when its reasoning and commitments are coherent.

| Dimension and weight | What strong work shows |
|---|---|
| Business problem and capability reasoning, 15% | Clear outcomes, legitimate variation and scope boundaries |
| Architecture and transition coherence, 20% | Consistent authority, interfaces, intermediate states and retirement conditions |
| Economics and roadmap feasibility, 20% | Correct arithmetic, explicit assumptions, dependencies and honest scope changes |
| Governance, risk and adoption, 15% | Accountable decisions, operational ownership and proportionate assurance |
| Research and measurement, 15% | Relevant sources, critical synthesis, stable measures and limits on attribution |
| Communication and revision, 15% | Traceable board brief, clear defence and a coherent response to both change cards |

Some defects need correction whatever the total score: unauthorised data access, unsupported claims of safe retirement, hidden double counting, or a roadmap that exceeds its funding. Use the rubric as feedback for revision, not as a way to average away a serious flaw.

## Prepare the defence

Give a ten-minute board presentation, followed by questions. Lead with the decision required and its consequence. Be ready to explain one rejected option, one assumption that could reverse the recommendation, one business risk during transition and one concrete result that would support rollout.

Then defend the technical appendix to a panel of architects. Trace one important operation through customer identity resolution, workforce access, data authority, failure, recovery and measurement. Explain where a contractual or organisational condition is needed for the technical design to work. This is where enterprise architecture meets the systems that carry out its promises.

## Exercises

17.1 Using the package-by-year tables in Chapter 11, check that the €7.2 million revision satisfies both its package totals and the revised annual limits. Then suppose the board insists on cutting every year's limit by 20%, to €2.4 million, €2.8 million and €2.0 million. Show where the revised plan breaks these limits, propose a schedule that meets them, and state what is delayed. Finally, identify the scheduling information still missing once the arithmetic agrees.

17.2 Respond to the acquisition without merging legal party, service site, contract and access entitlement into one identifier.

17.3 Write a board recommendation of no more than 250 words that names one conditional approval and the results required to release the next investment stage.

17.4 Design the decision criteria for Gate 2. For each criterion, state the measure, the threshold and the action if it is not met.

## Key points

- A strong recommendation connects strategy to systems through explicit responsibilities, operable transitions, supporting analysis and revisable investment commitments.
- Every board commitment should be traceable to the analysis behind it.
- Review gates are useful only when their results can change the decision.
- Change cards test whether the architecture can absorb new constraints honestly, without hiding lost promises.
