IT ARCHITECTURE SERIES — REVIEW RECORD
Revised edition, October 2026

WHAT WAS DONE
The three books were rewritten as self-contained textbooks: every lab listing, dataset and template is
printed in the book, and the content describes fictional teaching organisations only. The revision ran in
four rounds. After each round, separate reviewers who had not edited the books read every manuscript file,
ran the listings, recomputed worked numbers, checked a sample of references online and inspected the PDFs.
Their findings drove the next round.

CHECKS IN THIS EDITION
  Every Python listing in the three books runs with python3 -I (Python 3.13); every printed output that
    follows a listing matches the program's output exactly. The practice/ copies equal the printed listings.
  Book 2's SQL was run on PostgreSQL 18.6 scratch instances, including concurrent sessions and a real
    logical-replication publication and subscription for the Chapter 13 write fence.
  Exercises and answers match one to one (Book 1: 137, Book 2: 86, Book 3: 97).
  Every figure and citation key resolves; no reference is defined without being cited.
  New and changed sources were checked online before they were added.
  Contents and index page numbers are taken from the exported PDF and verified on each build.
  Figure text prints at 6.8 pt or larger.

LIMITS
  No human expert, academic or learner has reviewed this edition. The reviews above were automated and
    should not be read as an endorsement.
  Some details rest on secondary sources: the ArchiMate 4 concept counts (the author's published summary),
    COBIT 2019 details (ISACA articles), and the attribution of the TIME model and master-data styles to
    Gartner, which is stated without a citation.
  Product documentation (cloud services, PostgreSQL, Kafka, OpenTelemetry) changes; the books cite the
    versions current in October 2026.
  The PDFs are the checked layout. Word pagination differs if the fonts (Nimbus Sans, DejaVu Sans Mono) are
    not installed.
  Some chapters still end with a short page, because every chapter starts on a new page, and a few long
    listings that are kept on one page leave a gap before them.
